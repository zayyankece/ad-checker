Airworthiness Directives; Leonardo S.p.A. Helicopters

AGENCY: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), DOT.

ACTION: Notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The FAA proposes to supersede Airworthiness Directive (AD) 
2024-18-01, which applies to Leonardo S.p.A. Model A119 and AW119 MKII 
helicopters. AD 2024-18-01 requires repetitive inspections of certain 
collective stick torque tube assemblies for deficiencies and corrective 
action, as applicable. Since the FAA issued AD 2024-18-01, an improved 
collective stick torque tube assembly was designed, and replacement 
instructions were issued. This proposed AD would require repetitive 
inspections of certain collective stick torque tube assemblies and, 
depending on the results of the inspection, replacement of affected 
parts. This proposed AD would also require modification of the 
helicopter through replacement of an affected part with a serviceable 
part, which would constitute terminating action for the repetitive 
inspections. This proposed AD would also prohibit installation of an 
affected part on any helicopter. The FAA is proposing this 
airworthiness directive (AD) to address the unsafe condition on these 
products.

Background

    The FAA issued AD 2024-18-01, Amendment 39-22835 (89 FR 80993, 
October 7, 2024), (AD 2024-18-01), for all Leonardo S.p.A. Model A119 
and AW119 MKII helicopters. AD 2024-18-01 was prompted by an MCAI 
originated by EASA, which is the Technical Agent for the Member States 
of the European Union. EASA issued EASA AD 2023-0035, dated February 
10, 2023 (EASA AD 2023-0035) to correct an unsafe condition identified 
as reports of abnormal rotations on the collective stick torque tube 
assemblies due to erroneous manufacturing.
    AD 2024-18-01 requires repetitive inspections of certain collective 
stick torque tube assemblies for deficiencies and corrective action, as 
applicable. The FAA issued AD 2024-18-01 to address abnormal rotations 
on the collective torque tube assemblies.

Actions Since AD 2024-18-01 Was Issued

    Since the FAA issued AD 2024-18-01, EASA superseded EASA AD 2023-
0035 and issued EASA AD 2026-0045, dated March 4, 2026 (EASA AD 2026-
0045) (also referred to as the MCAI). The MCAI states that since EASA 
AD 2023-0035 was issued, an improved collective stick torque tube 
assembly was designed, and replacement instructions were issued. The 
unsafe condition, if not addressed, could lead to reduced control of 
the helicopter, which could result in a forced landing, damage to the 
helicopter, and injury to occupants.
    You may examine the MCAI in the AD docket at regulations.gov under 
Docket No. FAA-2026-11807.

[[Page 65139]]

Material Incorporated by Reference Under 1 CFR Part 51

    The FAA reviewed EASA AD 2026-0045, which specifies procedures for 
repetitive inspections of the collective stick torque tube assembly for 
any relative rotation and, depending on the inspection results, 
replacement of the affected parts with serviceable parts. EASA AD 2026-
0045 also specifies procedures for modification of the helicopter 
through the replacement of affected parts with serviceable parts within 
a specified compliance time. This replacement of an affected part with 
a serviceable part constitutes terminating action for the repetitive 
inspections. EASA AD 2026-0045 also prohibits the installation of 
affected parts.
    This material is reasonably available because the interested 
parties have access to it through their normal course of business or by 
the means identified in the ADDRESSES section.

FAA's Determination

    These products have been approved by the civil aviation authority 
(CAA) of another country and are approved for operation in the United 
States. Pursuant to the FAA's bilateral agreement with this State of 
Design Authority, that authority has notified the FAA of the unsafe 
condition described in the MCAI referenced above. The FAA is issuing 
this NPRM after determining that the unsafe condition described 
previously is likely to exist or develop on other products of the same 
type design.

Proposed AD Requirements in This NPRM

    This proposed AD would require accomplishing the actions specified 
in EASA AD 2026-0045, described previously, as incorporated by 
reference, except for any differences identified as exceptions in the 
regulatory text of this proposed AD.

Explanation of Required Compliance Information

    In the FAA's ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency of the AD 
process, the FAA developed a process to use some CAA ADs as the primary 
source of information for compliance with requirements for 
corresponding FAA ADs. The FAA has been coordinating this process with 
manufacturers and CAAs. As a result, the FAA proposes to incorporate 
EASA AD 2026-0045 by reference in the FAA final rule. This proposed AD 
would, therefore, require compliance with EASA AD 2026-0045 in its 
entirety through that incorporation, except for any differences 
identified as exceptions in the regulatory text of this proposed AD. 
Using common terms that are the same as the heading of a particular 
section in EASA AD 2026-0045 does not mean that operators need comply 
only with that section. For example, where the AD requirement refers to 
``all required actions and compliance times,'' compliance with this AD 
requirement is not limited to the section titled ``Required Action(s) 
and Compliance Time(s)'' in EASA AD 2026-0045. Material required by 
EASA AD 2026-0045 for compliance will be available at regulations.gov 
under Docket No. FAA-2026-11807 after the FAA final rule is published.

Authority for This Rulemaking

    Title 49 of the United States Code specifies the FAA's authority to 
issue rules on aviation safety. Subtitle I, section 106, describes the 
authority of the FAA Administrator. Subtitle VII, Aviation Programs, 
describes in more detail the scope of the Agency's authority.
    The FAA is issuing this rulemaking under the authority described in 
Subtitle VII, Part A, Subpart III, Section 44701: General requirements. 
Under that section, Congress charges the FAA with promoting safe flight 
of civil aircraft in air commerce by prescribing regulations for 
practices, methods, and procedures the Administrator finds necessary 
for safety in air commerce. This regulation is within the scope of that 
authority because it addresses an unsafe condition that is likely to 
exist or develop on products identified in this rulemaking action.

(a) Comments Due Date

    The FAA must receive comments on this airworthiness directive 
(AD) by November 30, 2026.

(b) Affected ADs

    This AD replaces AD 2024-18-01, Amendment 39-22835 (89 FR 80993, 
October 7, 2024) (AD 2024-18-01).

(c) Applicability

    This AD applies to Leonardo S.p.A. Model A119 and AW119 MKII 
helicopters, certificated in any category, as identified in European 
Union Aviation Safety Agency AD 2026-0045, dated March 4, 2026 (EASA 
AD 2026-0045).

(d) Subject

    Joint Aircraft System Component (JASC) Code 6700: Rotorcraft 
flight control.

(e) Unsafe Condition

    This AD was prompted by occurrences of abnormal rotations on the 
collective stick torque tube assemblies due to erroneous 
manufacturing. The unsafe condition, if not detected and corrected, 
could lead to reduced control of the helicopter, which could result 
in a forced landing, damage to the helicopter, and injury to 
occupants.

(f) Compliance

    Comply with this AD within the compliance times specified, 
unless already done.

(g) Requirements

    Except as specified in paragraphs (h) and (i) of this AD: Comply 
with all required actions and compliance times specified in and in 
accordance with EASA AD 2026-0045.

(h) Exceptions to EASA AD 2026-0045

    (1) Where EASA AD 2026-0045 refers to its effective date, this 
AD requires using the effective date of this AD.
    (2) Where EASA AD 2026-0045 refers to April 14, 2021 [the 
effective date of EASA AD 2021-0096], or where EASA AD 2026-0045 
refers to February 24, 2023 [the effective date of EASA AD 2023-
0035], this AD requires using the effective date of November 12, 
2024 [the effective date of AD 2024-18-01].
    (3) Where EASA AD 2026-0045 requires compliance in terms of 
flight hours, this AD requires using hours time-in-service.
    (4) This AD does not adopt the ``Remarks'' section of EASA AD 
2026-0045.

(i) No Reporting or Returning of Parts Requirement

    Although the material referenced in EASA AD 2026-0045 specifies 
submitting certain information or returning parts to the 
manufacturer, this AD does not include those requirements.
